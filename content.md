## Statement

My work begins with a simple belief: academic and technical practice matter most when they create room for collaboration, curiosity, and meaningful engagement with communities.

## Now

---
title: Memory, identity, place.
---

I’m exploring memory cultures and immersive narratives of place: how maps, film, interactive media, and emerging technologies can preserve, interpret, and communicate lived experience.

Current work includes Memory Mapping, interactive three-dimensional landscapes for personal memory, and Kashiwa After Dark, a spatial ethnography of youth culture, public space, and the city at night.

## Teaching

---
title: Learning by making, together.
---

At Reitaku’s Faculty of Engineering, I bring design, storytelling, technology, and community engagement into the same room. My courses include programming, data visualization, GIS, statistics, UI/UX, and the Global Engineer studio.

I also serve as Vice Director of Reitaku’s International Center.

## Before Reitaku

---
title: Twenty-five years at UCLA.
---

At UCLA, I led research, teaching, and public-engagement initiatives in geographic information systems, digital mapping, and the urban humanities. I helped develop the Urban Humanities Initiative and founded the International Humanitarian Mapathon.

Earlier work included spatial data visualization at the Office of Advanced Research Computing and information technology leadership at UCLA’s Center for Neighborhood Knowledge.
